**Important interpretation:** The filtered yaw near 0° is mainly the result of the firmware's `GYRO_Z_DEADZONE 0.3f` in stationary conditions. Do **not** present 0.003° or −0.011° as intrinsic standalone MPU6050 yaw stability. The raw-bias-subtracted integral drifts 0.458° and 0.801° respectively. This is a stationary, short-term integrated gyro heading, not independent absolute-heading accuracy.

The gyro zero-rate calibration completed in ~10s elapsed since boot (5s warm-up plus 4,000 samples). The printed start bias was **−1.508°/s STILL** and **−1.502°/s SHADOW**. Residual mean corrected gyro rate averaged **+0.00254°/s STILL** and **+0.00446°/s SHADOW**, consistent in magnitude with the integrated yaw drift.

Both trials show greater positive residual drift later in the 180s interval. ## Processor, MPU, I2C and UART implications

- **TIM11 CPU:** Max total TIM11 time rises from **968** to **2306 CPU cycles** when synthetic PID work is present; the PID section alone reaches **1309 cycles** (~13.64µs). At nominal 96MHz, total peak ISR demand is ~1.0% versus ~2.4% of each 1ms time slot. Both modes report zero TIM11 late/gap, thus the 1kHz ISR is not the bottleneck under the tested synthetic workload.
- **Actual MPU throughput:** ~998.2–998.6Hz, a small deviation from the target 1kHz; 60Hz transport is separate. MCU-reported transaction max ~133–134µs; I2C boot chose nominal **400,000Hz** in both sessions with successful WHO_AM_I=0x68, no fallback. At least one SHADOW raw completion window recorded a **2221µs maximum gap** despite zero recorded stale control ticks. Do not interpret `stale=0` as proof of no sub-3ms acquisition gaps.
- **UART risk:** Real average accepted-byte rate ~4.15–4.19kB/s, well under the 11520B/s physical 8N1 ceiling, yet cumulative TX peak hits **94–96%** of the 1024B ring because multiple 1Hz diagnostic rows are emitted together. No drops occurred, but **this workload no longer satisfies the prior benchmark's ≤768B queue headroom criterion**, even though the host's IMU-specific checks return PASS. Stagger or compact system 1Hz logs; then repeat the 180s check and enforce a peak-queue criterion in the soak report. Do not assume 80Hz is safe with these larger messages.
- **Gyro raw quantization/repeats:** In the 1Hz raw-sample windows, ~74.4% (STILL) / ~71.6% (SHADOW) consecutive raw values are identical. With ±2000°/s range and stationary input, repeated quantized readings are plausible; they are **not by themselves conclusive evidence of stale samples**. Sequence, timestamps and DATA_RDY must remain the primary freshness indicators. The first 1s noise window included ~1855–1859 raw samples because its accumulator was not cleared at SOAK start; do not use it as a precisely isolated first-second window.
- **IR:** Six channels are nonzero/near-zero and scan counters advance at approximately 1kHz; the static fixture cannot establish wall distance accuracy, cross-talk or performance on a speed-run. Compare emitter-on minus ambient and known-distance targets separately.

## Next actions prioritized

1. **P0 — Protect USART queue:** record and check queue **peak** (not only instantaneous queue depth) in `imu_soak_pipeline.py`; stagger/compress the many 1Hz `SYS_*` / `IM*` rows, or lower background health output. Keep 60Hz yaw stream and 1kHz IMU acquisition independent. Re-run and target max queue ≤768B at 115200 baud.
2. **P0 — Measure MPU die temperature:** extend async I2C to read temperature without disturbing the 1kHz gyro scheduling, log timestamp-aligned °C; repeat cold/warm static drift studies and correlate residual corrected gyro mean with temperature.
3. **P0 — Refresh stat window at exact SOAK start:** clear 1s raw-noise accumulation at start and report min/P95/P99/max **per-sample I2C latency** and interval gaps, not just cumulative maximum.
4. **P1 — Replicate trials:** at least three STILL/SHADOW paired trials per condition, randomized/counterbalanced order and comparable mounting/thermal/battery conditions. Assess drift confidence intervals; avoid attributing differences to PID based on two sequential runs.
5. **P1 — Compare DLPF 42Hz vs 98Hz with both static noise and measured turn response:** lower static drift alone does not prove high-speed steering suitability.
6. **P1 — Mechanically verify LIVE:** wheels up, independent VM kill switch, bounded PWM and actual encoder pulses required for acceptance. In the uploaded run, **only STILL and SHADOW were performed; motor load/EMI is not qualified**.
7. **P2 — IR wall calibration:** record ambient/emitter-differenced values, sensor saturation, turn entry/exit timing and consistency at known wall distances.

## Reproducibility and limitations

Report derived directly from committed hardware CSV/JSON data. Clock frequencies are **RCC-derived nominal**, not measured with an oscilloscope. No independent gyro temperature, external yaw ground truth, supply voltage/current, actual electrical PWM waveform or dynamic maze run is available. Software PASS means telemetry and scheduler counters met configured tests only, not that all Micromouse high-speed motion requirements are met.
